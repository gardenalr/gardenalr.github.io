# **The Origin of the Surname Gardenal**

For a long time, the origins of the Gardenal surname remained based on oral information passed down from generation to generation. However, the landscape of genealogical research has evolved significantly in recent decades. The digitization of parish and notarial records, the advancement of artificial intelligence that facilitated research in several languages, including Latin, combined with historical studies focused on the territories that made up the former Republic of Venice (697 - 1797), allowed the retrieval of studies and documents that helped to reconstruct the trajectory of the surname.

### **1. The "Son of Gardani" Hypothesis**

In the early 2000s, driven by the desire to recover family memory, members of the Gardenal family published a first study on the history of the surname. On that occasion, the hypothesis was adopted that *Gardenal* would be a surname of patronymic origin, meaning "son or descendant of Gardani" (a derivation of medieval proper names from northern Italy).

We should be immensely grateful for this pioneering effort, whose enthusiasm aroused curiosity and laid the foundations for future research. However, the analysis based on current studies and documents directs us to discard the "son of Gardani" thesis. As demonstrated later, this association stemmed from the methodology of old commercial heraldic databases from the 1990s and 2000s, which grouped visually similar surnames (such as *Gardini* or *Gardani with Gardinal and Gardenal*) into the same trunk, without any common linguistic root or historical facts demonstrating the transition.

### **2. Other Discarded Hypotheses: Origin in "Garden"**

Gardini and Gardani have Germanic roots, meaning Garden or protected enclosure, which also suggests other theoretical explanations based on more superficial research, relating the surname to a toponymic (local) or occupational origin also linked to the word "garden" (*garden* in English or *jardin* in French), or related to the gardenia flower, suggesting that the first bearers would be garden cultivators.

This hypothesis does not withstand the analysis of Northeastern Italian dialects. In the Venetian language and in the variants used in the Istrian peninsula, the word for garden is *xiardim* or *giardino*, never *garden*. The graphic similarity with the Anglo-Saxon (English) root *garden* is nothing more than a false cognate without backing in the characteristics of the languages or in the medieval documents of the region.

### **3. The Origin in *Cardinale* and the Evolution in the Venetian Language**

Cardinale, the origin of the surname, was demonstrated by Marino Bonifacio in 2010. Marino is a well-known Italian academic and author (born in Pirano, 1941) dedicated to the study of dialects, toponymy, and genealogy. He is famous for writing works such as the “Dizionario dei cognomi di Trieste, dell'Istria, del Quarnero e della Dalmazia”, which maps the historical origin of thousands of surnames from that region.

According to Marino's research, the original meaning emerged as a nickname applied to individuals in the service of cardinals of the Catholic Church. Cardinale, the highest Catholic position after the Pope. As a second possibility, a toponymic origin, applied to peasants who resided and worked on Church lands, and finally as an indication of distinctive characteristics of an individual who had a posture or clothing associated with a Cardinal. It is important to remember that the Catholic Church was for centuries a large property owner, which would justify the birth of different lineages of the Gardenal surname. Therefore, based on this rationale, all hypotheses are related to Cardinale, with Gardenal being an archaic dialectal evolution of Cardinale (Cardinal), passing through the variations Cardinale, Cardinal, Cardenal, Gardinal, Gardenàl. [1](#6.-bibliographical-references)

The phonetic evolution occurred in the Veneto and in the dialects of Istria, where the consonant C at the beginning of words frequently voiced to G, transforming *Cardinale* into *Gardinale*. Finally, the drop of the unaccented final vowel (apocope) consolidated the form *Gardenal* or *Gardinal*. [1](#6.-bibliographical-references)

The surname proves the linguistic concordances among Istria, Friuli, Veneto, and Trentino.

### **4. Historical Chronology, Documentary Mapping and Migrations**

On the Italian peninsula, it is found in Salpi, Foggia, Apulia, where in 1279 a Gualterius dictus Cardinalis is witnessed. [1](#6.-bibliographical-references)

In Trentino, for example, those in charge of supplying water to the cardinals during the Council of Trent (from 1545 onwards) were locally called *Gardenài*. [3](#6.-bibliographical-references)

Already with the spelling Gardenal, documentary research reveals that the surname was already in Istria about two and a half centuries before the surname was recorded in Friuli (where it appears in the Colombara of Aquileia from 1693 with *Gioseffo* and his brother *Gardinali*). [1](#6.-bibliographical-references)

In **Pula, Istria (1448 / 1451), Republic of Venice**, today part of Croatia, in a will dated February 5, 1451, the presbyter **Zane Gardenal** is mentioned, also designated in previous records from 1448 as **Presbiter Iohannes Cardinal**. In the Venetian language, Zane is the spoken form for the Italian name Giovanni, while Iohannes is the Latin form used to write the same name, both being John in English. In 1945 there was still a Gardenal family in Pula consisting of two people. [1](#6.-bibliographical-references) [4](#6.-bibliographical-references)

**In Due Castelli and Rovinj, Istria (1494 / 1495), Republic of Venice**, today also part of Croatia: The progenitor of the Rovinj lineage is **Antonio**, son of the late *Antonio Gardenal* (known as *Beliaco* and *Gaiardo de dò Castei*, meaning from *Due Castelli*). Antonio is proven in Rovinj since 1495. [1](#6.-bibliographical-references)

In 1595, the *Cardinal* and *Fiorin-Cardenal* families are recorded in Rovinj. [1](#6.-bibliographical-references) [5](#6.-bibliographical-references)

In 1613, *Bortolo Gardenal* of Rovinj married in Spalato (Split). [1](#6.-bibliographical-references)

The historical Rovinj branch became extinct around 1850. [1](#6.-bibliographical-references)

**Still in Rovinj and Surroundings (1580 to 1610):**

Following the path indicated by Marino, I carried out additional research in Istrian parish documents and found some more records still in the 16th century.

* On January 31, 1580, Zorzi Gardenal married Francesca de Rocho, therefore Zorzi was born around 1560, son of a Gardenal father born around 1540. In the Venetian language, Zorzi is the form for the name Giorgio in Italian. [2](#6.-bibliographical-references)
* On November 3, 1610, Bartola Gardenal married, daughter of another Zorzi Gardenal and Domenega, born around 1570. [2](#6.-bibliographical-references)

### **5. Settlement in Veneto**

In the 15th century, there were land and sea routes and the bearers of the Gardenal surname, although it is not possible to state definitively, may have moved from Istria and Friuli to a more central direction in Veneto, or other lineages may have emerged directly in Veneto at some point, following the connection with the Cardinals of the Catholic Church.

It is a fact that from the 17th and 18th centuries onwards, the surname is identified in the interior of Veneto, more specifically in the province of Treviso, where they settled in locations such as Conegliano, San Vendemiano, Mareno di Piave, Vazzola, Codognè, and Oderzo.

Around 1740, Pietro Gardenal was born, the oldest known ancestor of the lineage that would migrate to Tietê, São Paulo, Brazil in 1897, with Pietro being the great-grandfather of Giuseppe Gardenal.
